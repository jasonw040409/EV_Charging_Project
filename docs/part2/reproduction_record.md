## Equation and path audit

- `charging.py` maps grid kW to SOC via `s[i+1]=s[i]+eta*p[i]*dt/capacity`; equality enforces grid kWh `capacity*(target-initial)/eta`, while battery kWh is grid kWh times efficiency. Real-case target is recalculated from each assumed initial SOC and recorded delivered grid energy. Both price dot product and path reconstruction use hours. Four methods share physical constraints. The two-stage cost-only solve first minimizes cost then minimizes power concentration subject to cost at most $1e-8 above its first-stage value; solver_seconds reports the second stage only.
- SOC array length N+1 and price/power length N, all stored path dynamics, energy, bounds, final SOC, expenses and calendar values were recomputed for all 236 paths. Maximum SOC reconstruction difference was 8.89e-16. All 236 result rows match their frozen counterparts on eight reported metrics, maximum difference zero in this environment. This includes 64 synthetic sensitivity rows and 12 weight rows; all 36 post-evaluation temperature/history outputs also match. The 12 weight paths are new; the frozen pack saved only 224 paths.
- S2 at 60%: 45 power intervals and 46 SOC boundaries, 10.013 grid kWh, 60% to 75.0195%. Power-only and combined schedules differ by up to 2.3505 kW. S3 at 60%: 38/39, 5.257 kWh, 60% to 67.8855%; its power-only/combined maximum difference is 1.40e-8 kW and the 70% proxy is inactive. S2 at 75% requires 90.0195%, exceeding the 90% cap, and is recorded as infeasible. Flat-price cost-only power is uniform within 1e-5 kW. See `verification.json` for per-method evidence.
- `aging.py` implements the documented fractional loss, seconds and kelvin coefficients, preserves prior loss through squared-loss continuation, integrates the full N intervals including idle time with four-node quadrature, and reports fraction times 1e6 as ppm. Existing tests cover constant-stress closed form, history splitting and timestep consistency. This is a code/specification check, not an independent primary-source review or evidence of a pack lifetime effect.

## Exclusions, selection and outstanding issues

- Five assumed-SOC scenarios are analytically infeasible; none is a solver failure. The new ledger distinguishes `invalid_record`, `invalid_scenario`, `infeasible_assumed_scenario`, `optimizer_or_path_failure`, and `aging_evaluator_failure`; reasons are retained. The original flagged control is still solved and visible but excluded from clean paired comparisons. There are 37 complete unflagged pairs (14/14/9 by starting SOC), with individual changes retained including zero and adverse values. Tiny negative cost premiums near 1e-8 dollars are solver/tie-break tolerance, not real savings.
- The frozen 100-record candidate ledger contains 91 eligible candidates under its existing screening, of which 10 were selected and 81 were not. This is not a demonstrated representative population. Expansion to 50 remains unexecuted: the handoff calls for the data owner and implementation owner to agree and freeze expansion selection/provenance rules before solving. The current code accepts a separate configured cohort; it does not silently change the reference or assert the required human agreement. No new data was fetched and no credentials were used.
- The historical runner hardcodes ten additions and its narrative contains fixed counts; the new runner reads the cohort and experiment config and its summary derives counts and distributions from outputs. It intentionally does not alter that historical runner or its frozen summaries. Sensitivity families retained: baseline, flat/late prices, windows, threshold, start/target SOC, resolution, weights, and temperature/history on fixed paths.
- Numerical acceptance uses optimal status and the existing thresholds (energy/power and mixed constraint residual <1e-6, SOC/target <1e-8). These are primal checks, not a numerical KKT/dual audit. Do not interpret mixed-unit residuals as one physical quantity. The score threshold is assumed, and calendar estimates are model-specific and provisional.

## Timing scope and remaining human checks

Five recorded repetitions per resolution and each of three optimizers follow one unrecorded warm-up per combination on the same 12-hour synthetic case. The outer `perf_counter` covers the complete `optimize` call, including validation, CVXPY graph construction, canonicalization and solve(s), excluding aging, metrics and output. Each solve constructs a new problem; it is not a cached-solver benchmark. `solver_seconds` is the final solver stage only, and the inner reported wall scope starts after initial model construction. The immediate heuristic is not timed. Runtime includes normal machine noise, and resolution also changes the end-of-slot SOC proxy. On this macOS arm64 machine, Python 3.12.8, 8 logical CPUs, combined median outer times were approximately 0.0109/0.0116/0.0132/0.0206 s for 60/30/15/5 minutes; these are exploratory, not scaling guarantees. Exact CPU model and memory were unavailable under the local system-query restriction. Environment/package details and every repetition are saved.
